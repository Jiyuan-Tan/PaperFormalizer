The paper has been verified in Lean, and the candidates are declarations its formalization proved. They are listed as an INDEX: every candidate, one line each, under the Lean file that proves it (a path from the toolkit root, your working directory). A line reads `- <name> [<what the scan knows>] <the start of its statement>`; its `candidate_id` is `{{PREFIX}}<name>`. In the brackets: `F` / `T` — its statement leans on Mathlib alone (Foundations-shaped) or on a domain library or the toolkit (Tricks-shaped); `used by N` — N other candidates' proofs use it (a helper usually enters with the result it serves, yet a much-used one may be a main result itself); `recurs: <papers>` — the same statement, or the same shape of hypotheses and conclusion, appears in those other papers of the owner; `paper-shaped` — its Lean is stated over the paper's own setting; `unused: <names>` — hypotheses its proof never uses; `stated in the paper` — one of the paper's own lemmas or propositions (propose it only when it is standard or general); `deferred before` — the owner deferred the same entry in an earlier paper.

Work as follows. Read the index and pick the results a textbook of the field would state on their own or the owner would reuse in the next paper: a paper yields few of these, and most of its declarations are steps of its own argument. Open the Lean file of each one you consider and read the declaration (its Lean statement as proved) and its docstring before you write its entry; search the toolkit and the base libraries (Grep) for the nearest existing declaration, since no search hits are given. Return an item ONLY for each result you propose, at most {{MAX_PROPOSALS}}, the strongest first. Every candidate you do not name is set aside; return no `skip` items. `recurrence_confirmed` is `[]`.

An accepted entry enters the toolkit by one of two paths:
- `move`: the result moves as proved, with the helper lemmas proving it. `content` says exactly what its Lean statement proves: not more, not less.
- `generalize`: the general statement is built in the toolkit from scratch and the paper's result is re-proved as its corollary. Use it when the mathematics is general but the Lean is stated over the paper's own setting: its own types or constructions, a reading of the paper's wording, the paper's assumptions. `content` is then the GENERAL statement a textbook would give — over arbitrary objects of the right kind, each hypothesis stated in its own terms, nothing of the paper's notation or setting — such that the paper's Lean statement is a direct special case of it. A `paper-shaped` entry always takes `generalize`. So does one with `unused: …`: its general statement leaves those hypotheses out.

A classical, standard or named result, or one that recurs in the owner's other papers, is proposed even when its Lean is paper-specific — on the `generalize` path; being paper-shaped is never by itself a reason to pass over it.
