The paper is planned in Lean but not proved yet. The candidates are results the paper relies on WITHOUT proving: results it cites from other works (`statement` is the result as the paper states it, `source.citation` the work it cites, `context.source_text` the cited work's own text when it was fetched). Unbuilt, each stays an assumption of the paper. An accepted entry is BUILT in the toolkit from scratch — scaffolded, proved and reviewed by the study pipeline before the paper's proofs — and the paper then uses it instead of assuming it. The paper's own lemmas and theorems are never candidates here.

`path` is always `generalize`: an entry is built in general form. `content` states the result as the cited work states it (prefer `context.source_text` over the paper's paraphrase), in its own generality — the form a textbook gives, not the paper's special case. `reference` names the cited work with its theorem or section number. Building is substantial for a deep result or one needing theory the base libraries lack: say so in `reuse_evidence` so the owner can weigh the cost.

Each candidate is shown in full, one JSON object per line. `recurrence` lists the same cited theorem in the owner's other papers; `search_hits` are the closest declarations found in the base libraries and the toolkit — read them for `nearest_existing`, and `skip` a candidate one of them already states, naming it in `reuse_evidence`. Judge every candidate exactly once, by `candidate_id`: `propose` or `skip`. For a `skip`, put the one-line reason in `reuse_evidence`, leave the other text fields empty strings, `nearest_existing` and `recurrence_confirmed` empty, `new_module_overview` and `new_topic` null, `qualifies_by` "judgement" and `path` "move".
